2Why it happens

The pin is right. The route ends on the wrong road.

  • The expressway deck sits almost exactly above the canteen's road: 18 m vs 17 m away. On a flat map they are one line, so the route's end snaps to the expressway.
  • Bikes can't use it, so navigation sends the rider to the nearest legal road, N3. The rider stops there, across the highway.
  • The right road costs the rider only +52 m and ~40 s: one left turn off N3. Miss that turn and it's a 2.5 km detour.

Hypothesis built from OpenStreetMap road data and OSRM routes. Confirm with Pathao's geocoder and trip logs.

3The fix: Smart Drop-off

Better GPS logic on the backend, plus coordination between passenger and rider

A
End the ride where bikes can stop Backend

Even when the pin is right, never end the route on a road bikes can't use: expressways, ramps, no-motorcycle roads. Use the nearest road they can.

B
Saved spot + your own tip Passenger

The passenger confirms the spot with one tap and can add a tip in their own words. The app asks to remember both, so next time there's no question.

C
Rider sees it before accepting Rider

The spot and the tip come with the ride request, plus a short Bangla reminder. If the spot is on a highway, the rider is reminded to stop safely.

① Backend fix→② Saved spot + tips→③ Learn from trips

Why management should approve it

  • It protects commuters, our most frequent riders. A daily route that fails half the time teaches them to open Uber first. At 2 trips a day, that's ~22 bad drop-offs a month for one person.
  • It's small and proven. It uses map data and trip GPS we already have, step ① ships silently on the server, and food delivery apps already save rider notes per address.
  • Better GPS alone won't fix it. The pin is already right. The route end and the way in are the problem, at every venue beside a flyover or expressway.

How we'll know it worked

  • Primary: drop-off error, the distance from where the trip ended to the confirmed destination. Target: median under 25 m.
  • Also: "wrong drop-off" tickets, rider–passenger calls in the last 2 minutes, and 30-day commuter retention.
  • Guardrails: rider trip time, fare and acceptance rate. 4-week A/B test in Dhaka.

Open questions before this ships

  • How many Dhaka destinations snap to no-stop roads? One trip-log query sizes it.
  • Does our map provider give us bridge, layer and access tags, or must we derive them?
  • Can riders enter every gate (cantonment and DOHS rules)? Who checks gates that passengers suggest?
  • Does the rider need an incentive for the extra turn-in?
